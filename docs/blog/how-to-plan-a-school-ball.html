How to Plan a School Ball: A Production Checklist

Between June and September, schools across Christchurch and Canterbury run their balls. The committees that get it right start early and know what to ask for. We've done production for dozens of school balls around the region. This is the checklist we'd give you.

Start Early: 4 to 6 Months Out

Venues, DJs and production companies all get booked at the same time. Lock these in first:

  • Venue. Everything else depends on the space. The Air Force Museum, Wolfbrook Arena function rooms and hotel ballrooms fill up fast.
  • Budget. Set the number early. Production (sound, lighting, DJ) usually takes a solid chunk.
  • Production company. Talk to us early. We'll tell you what's realistic for your budget and venue, and lock in the date.

Sound

If people can't hear the music, the dance floor dies. A typical ball of 200 to 400 students needs a mid-to-large PA with subs.

  • PA sized to the venue and crowd
  • Wireless microphone for speeches and announcements
  • Foldback speaker if there are live performances
  • A sound engineer (strongly recommended for balls)

See our school ball production page for what we do.

Lighting

Lighting is what turns a function room into a ball.

  • Moving heads for the dance floor
  • Uplighting in your theme colours. Cheap to add, big visual impact.
  • Haze. Without it you can't see the beams. Most venues allow it, but check first.
  • Lasers, if the budget allows and the venue permits

We cover all of this on our lighting hire page.

DJ

A good school ball DJ reads the room, knows what works for a younger crowd and can handle the mic for announcements and awards. Ask:

  • Do they bring their own gear, or need a PA and monitors provided?
  • Can they handle speeches and announcements?
  • Do they have experience with school events?

We have DJs who do school balls regularly and know what works.

Venue Considerations

Check these before you sign anything:

  • Power. Is there enough for a full sound and lighting rig? Older venues sometimes struggle. We can check this for you.
  • Load-in access. Can we get a van or truck close to the door? Is there a loading dock? Stairs add time and cost.
  • Haze and smoke machines. Some venues have sensitive fire alarms. Ask upfront.
  • Noise restrictions. Venues near residential areas sometimes have sound level limits. We deal with this regularly.
  • Setup time. Allow at least 3 to 4 hours of bump-in for a full production.

The Week Before

Everything should be booked and confirmed. Final check:

  • Bump-in time and access with the venue
  • Run sheet (doors, speeches, awards, last song)
  • Must-play and do-not-play lists to the DJ
  • Main contact on the night for the production crew
  • Playlist or Spotify login if there's pre-DJ background music
